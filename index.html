Ice melt math

Ice season goes wrong in three places: the pellets thrown by the handful instead of by the grams the walk actually needs, the bag bought by habit instead of by division, and the chemistry picked by the label's promise instead of by the coldest night in the forecast. Ice melt math turns the walk into the grams, turns the grams into the bag, and turns the forecast into the chemistry. Exact arithmetic, every borrowed number labeled.

Open the calculators

The three tools

  • Melt plan - the walk area and the ice thickness become the grams of melt to spread.
  • Bag plan - the grams at a labeled bag size become the bags to buy and the kilograms left for the next storm.
  • Chemistry plan - the coldest night in the forecast becomes the melt chemistry that still works.

Labeled norms

Labeled norms: 40 g per m2 for a frost film, 80 for a thin glaze and 140 for a stubborn sheet, plus a labeled 10 kg bag; rock salt to -9 C, magnesium chloride to -15 C and calcium chloride to -29 C - all illustrative defaults you can change by measuring. Melt is for the film the shovel leaves behind, so clear the snow first. Chlorides burn paws and young concrete, and sweep up what the melt leaves. Real footing comes from boots and sand, not from chemistry.
